Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 August 2026

The short version

Mad Banana Media collects your information when you hand it over on purpose: filling in the signup form on this site, booking a call, subscribing to one of our newsletters, buying a place on the Newsletter Mastery Program, or emailing us. Everything else on this page is the detail behind that.

Your information is never sold, rented or traded.

Some pages on this site do carry analytics and advertising tracking, and this policy sets out exactly which pages, which tools, and what each one sees. The market pages on our subdomains carry none of it.

Who is responsible for your information

Mad Banana Media is the trading name of Chris Cerra, a sole trader based in the United Kingdom. Agreements are signed as "Chris Cerra (sole trader, trading as Mad Banana Media)". It is not a limited company.

Two newsletters sit under Mad Banana Media: RemoteBase at remotebase.co and Digital Nomad Trip Reports at digitalnomadtripreports.com. Alongside them is a consulting practice at madbananamedia.com, which advises businesses and individuals on newsletter strategy. The advice is strategy and ongoing counsel. It does not include building or running your newsletter for you.

Under UK data protection law, the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, Chris Cerra is the data controller for everything described here. He decides what is collected and why, and he is the person answerable for it.

What is collected, and why

When you visit this website

Four pages on madbananamedia.com load Google Tag Manager, container GTM-TBKWRFGD: the home page, the Newsletter Mastery Program page, the archived Cohort 1 version of that page, and the program thank you page. Tag Manager collects nothing by itself. It is a loader, and it decides which of the tools below run on those pages.

The other pages on this site, including this one, do not load Tag Manager and none of the tools below run on them.

On the four pages that do load it, the following run on every visit:

  • Google Analytics 4 (measurement ID G-4XM2M2EFSJ). Records which pages you view, how far you scroll, clicks on calls to action, clicks on email links, clicks through to the Stripe checkout, and completed purchases. It also receives your IP address, browser and device type, an approximate location worked out from your IP address, and the site that referred you.
  • Meta pixel (ID 1128321852604207). Sends Meta a record of your page view, and of specific actions: viewing programme content, making contact, starting checkout, and completing a purchase. Meta may match that activity to your Facebook or Instagram account. It is used to measure advertising and to build advertising audiences.
  • Microsoft Clarity (project vbiuuez4ah). Records your session on the page: mouse movement, scrolling, clicks and taps, which are combined into heatmaps and session replays used to work out which parts of a page are working.
  • A copy tag. If you select and copy text from one of those pages, the fact that you copied and the text you copied are recorded as an event.

These tools set cookies and similar identifiers on your device. There is currently no cookie consent banner on this site, so you can control them through your browser settings, your device's advertising controls, and the opt-out tools each provider offers.

Two more things happen on every page of this site.

The typeface loads from Google. Every page uses Space Grotesk, served by Google Fonts. Your browser has to request that file from Google's servers, which means your IP address and basic technical information about your browser and device reach Google. This happens as the page loads, before you have clicked anything, and Google handles what it receives under its own privacy policy.

The pages are served by GitHub Pages, which keeps standard server logs in the normal way.

The home page and the archived Cohort 1 page also embed a testimonial widget from testimonial.to. Loading that widget sends your IP address and browser information to testimonial.to.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests, UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f), in serving a working website and understanding how it is used. Where a cookie or similar identifier is set for analytics or advertising, consent is the correct basis and the note above applies.

When you visit one of the market pages

The market pages on subdomains of madbananamedia.com, such as motorsport.madbananamedia.com, carry no analytics, no advertising pixels and no cookies of their own. Nothing is stored on your device and no visit is logged. There is no cookie banner on those pages because there is nothing to consent to.

The Google Fonts point above still applies, because those pages use the same typeface. Beyond that, the only place to hand over information is the booking link.

When you use the signup form on this site

The signup form in the footer of every page, and the one at the top of the home page, sends your details to MailerLite, which runs the Mad Musings mailing list.

Collected: your name and your email address, plus the engagement data MailerLite records once you are on the list, such as whether an email was opened and which links were clicked.

Used for: sending you that mailing list.

Lawful basis: consent, UK GDPR Article 6(1)(a). Every email carries an unsubscribe link and it works immediately.

When you book a call

Booking links on this site take you to Cal.com.

Collected: your name, your email address, and your answers to a short set of qualification questions on the booking form about your situation. Cal.com also records the time and timezone of the booking so the appointment lands in the right slot.

Used for: holding the appointment, sending you the joining link and reminders, and preparing properly for the conversation so it is worth your time.

Lawful basis: taking steps at your request before entering into a contract, Article 6(1)(b). Where a call does not lead to us working together, the basis for keeping the record and following up is legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f), in responding to an enquiry you started.

When we speak on a call

Calls are recorded and transcribed using Fathom, so the conversation gets full attention instead of half of it going into note taking. The recording and transcript are used to write up notes, to follow up on what was agreed, and to improve how these conversations are run.

Recordings are not published, and they are not shared with anyone outside Mad Banana Media.

Lawful basis: consent, Article 6(1)(a). You can decline the recording, and you can ask for it to be deleted afterwards.

When you subscribe to a newsletter

RemoteBase and Digital Nomad Trip Reports both run on beehiiv.

Collected: your email address, anything else you choose to give at signup, and the engagement data beehiiv records automatically. That means whether an issue was opened, which links were clicked, and technical data such as IP address, approximate location and device type.

Used for: sending you the newsletter, understanding which issues land and which do not, and reporting audience numbers to sponsors.

Sponsor reporting is aggregate: subscriber counts, open rates, click rates. No sponsor is given your email address, and none is given your individual activity.

Lawful basis: consent, Article 6(1)(a). Every issue carries an unsubscribe link, it works immediately, and you never have to explain yourself to use it.

When you buy a place on the Newsletter Mastery Program

Payment is taken through Stripe. Your card details go to Stripe and are never seen or held by Mad Banana Media. What comes back is your name, your email address and a record of the purchase.

Some programme forms, such as the waitlist and the onboarding questionnaire, run on Tally. Tally holds whatever you type into them.

Used for: taking payment, running the programme, and the financial records a business is required to keep.

Lawful basis: performance of a contract, Article 6(1)(b), for the purchase and the programme itself. Legal obligation, Article 6(1)(c), for the financial records tax law requires.

When you email, and when we work together

Email runs on Google Workspace. Documents, notes and working files sit in Google Drive and Google Sheets. Enquiries and projects are tracked internally in Notion.

Collected: whatever is in your messages, plus your name, email address, the organisation you work with, and notes on what was discussed and agreed. For clients, that also covers the paperwork and invoicing a piece of work generates.

Lawful basis: performance of a contract where we are working together, Article 6(1)(b). Legitimate interests for correspondence and enquiry records, Article 6(1)(f), in running the business and keeping an accurate account of what was said. Legal obligation for the financial records tax law requires, Article 6(1)(c).

Information leaving the UK

Most of the services listed above are run by companies based outside the United Kingdom, mainly in the United States, so your information is transferred out of the UK.

Those transfers are made under the safeguards UK data protection law requires: the International Data Transfer Agreement, or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, and the UK extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified under it.

In plain terms, the provider is contractually bound to protect your information to a standard broadly equivalent to the one it would have here.

The principle is straightforward: information is kept for as long as there is a reason to keep it, then deleted.

Mailing list and newsletter records are kept for as long as you stay subscribed. When you unsubscribe, the provider keeps a record of that fact so you are not accidentally added back.

Financial and tax records are kept for the period UK tax law requires of a sole trader, whatever the state of the relationship.

Your rights

UK data protection law gives you the following rights over your information. They apply to everything described on this page.

  • Access. Ask for a copy of the information held about you, and an explanation of what is done with it.
  • Rectification. Have anything inaccurate corrected, and anything incomplete filled in.
  • Erasure. Ask for your information to be deleted, where there is no overriding reason to keep it.
  • Restriction. Ask for processing to be paused while a dispute about accuracy or grounds is sorted out.
  • Portability. Receive the information you provided in a common machine-readable format, or have it sent to someone else.
  • Objection. Object to processing based on legitimate interests. Where the processing is direct marketing, an objection is absolute and it stops.
  • Withdrawing consent. Where consent is the basis, such as the mailing lists, the newsletters or a call recording, you can withdraw it at any time. That does not undo what was lawfully done beforehand.

No automated decision-making or profiling with legal or similarly significant effects is carried out on your information.

To exercise any of these, email hi@madbananamedia.com. There is no charge, and a request is answered within one month. You may be asked to confirm your identity first, so that information is not handed to the wrong person.

If you are unhappy with how your information is handled

Raise it directly first, at hi@madbananamedia.com. Most things can be fixed quickly that way.

You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK's independent regulator for data protection, at ico.org.uk. You do not have to raise it here before going to them.

How your information is kept safe

Access to every service listed here is protected by strong, unique passwords and two-factor authentication where the provider supports it. Information is not stored on personal devices beyond what is needed to work, and it is not shared with anyone outside Mad Banana Media without a reason you would recognise.

No system is completely secure, and nobody honest claims otherwise. If a breach happened that put your rights at risk, you would be told, and so would the Information Commissioner's Office within the 72 hours the law allows.
